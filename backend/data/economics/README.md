# Economics references

These CSVs provide published crop prices, yields, production costs, and custom-hire rates for the economics loader in `acre/economics/data.py`. They contain 124 price rows, 472 yield rows, nine cost rows, and 12 custom-rate rows, retrieved on 2026-11-15 UTC. Missing values stay blank. These are historical references, not forecasts or farm-specific budgets.

Every output row contains `source_title`, `source_url`, `locator`, `retrieved`, and `origin`. The source locator records the observation years, original units, and selection within the source. `sources/` preserves the selected source observations, including source flags and PDF row text; `sources/manifest.json` records the original download URLs, sizes, and SHA-256 checksums.

## Sources and selection

- **USDA NASS prices:** [Agricultural Prices, July 2025](https://release.nass.usda.gov/reports/agpr0725.pdf), pages 43-44. Ten national crop series use the five calendar years 2020-2024: maize, upland cotton, all wheat, peanuts, rice, all barley, oats, sorghum, soybeans, and sunflower. These are NASS's simple averages of monthly prices, not marketing-year averages. The 2024 averages are preliminary in this release. `low`, `typical`, and `high` are the minimum, median, and maximum of those five annual values. The source values appear in each locator.
- **USDA NASS yields:** [Crop Production 2024 Summary, January 2025](https://esmis.nal.usda.gov/sites/default/release-files/k3569432s/nk324887m/qn59s0097/cropan25.pdf). The 2024 yield columns provide 274 rows across 14 crop series, including national totals and 260 state rows covering 43 states. Maize, sorghum, oats, barley, all wheat, winter wheat, other spring wheat, peanuts, canola, soybeans, rice, sunflower, all cotton, and upland cotton retain their source scope. Rice and sunflower use the all-class totals. Cotton yield is lint, not unginned seed cotton. Suppressed or unavailable state values are omitted. The selected year is fixed; newer releases are not mixed into this snapshot.
- **FAOSTAT:** [Producer Prices bulk download](https://bulks-faostat.fao.org/production/Prices_E_All_Data_(Normalized).zip), element 5532, annual observations only, and [Crops and livestock products bulk download](https://bulks-faostat.fao.org/production/Production_Crops_Livestock_E_All_Data_(Normalized).zip), yield element 5412. The import selects cereals, pulses, oilseeds, roots, and sugar crops listed in `ITEMS` in `import_sources.py`; it does not claim to cover fruit, vegetables, or every commodity. The selected source observations cover 2020-2024. Price rows use every available annual USD/tonne observation in that window; low/typical/high are minimum/median/maximum. A single observation has a typical value and blank low/high. Yield rows use the latest available observation, normally 2024; Mexico millet is 2023. FAO's kg/ha yields are divided by 1,000 to produce t/ha. FAO already supplies USD prices; this import performs no currency conversion. The raw producer-price unit column says `USD`; element 5532 identifies the denominator as tonne.
- **USDA ERS costs:** [Commodity Costs and Returns](https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/commodity-costs-and-returns), historical 2024 U.S. total rows for corn, soybeans, wheat, cotton, rice, sorghum, oats, barley, and peanuts. Each crop has exactly one `Total, costs listed` row. It includes operating and allocated overhead costs, including opportunity costs. Subtotals and individual cost items are preserved in the source extracts but are not added again. These are sector accounts, not cash-only budgets; see [ERS documentation](https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/commodity-costs-and-returns/documentation). ERS farm-resource regions are not states and have not been relabelled as states.
- **Iowa State University custom rates:** [2026 Iowa Farm Custom Rate Survey, A3-10](https://www.extension.iastate.edu/agdm/crops/pdf/a3-10.pdf), pages 2-3, Average Charge column. The downloaded file is the 2026 edition even though search results described 2025. Twelve selected operations map to the backend equipment vocabulary. Rates include fuel, operator labor, and machinery costs; spray materials are excluded. They apply only to `US-IA`. The rates describe custom hire rather than the cost of operating a farmer-owned machine.

## Scope, units, and gaps

Country price/yield row counts are: AR 0/23, BR 18/20, IN 0/21, FR 11/14, US 22/27, CN 13/24, NG 4/14, KE 20/22, AU 13/23, and MX 23/24. U.S. counts here are national rows; the 260 state yield rows are additional. `coverage.json` lists every imported region. `gaps.json` lists each imported national yield crop that lacks a price, each incomplete price window, and source-access failures.

Argentina and India have no annual USD producer-price observations in the selected FAOSTAT 2020-2024 crop window. Their prices remain absent. Nigeria has only 2020 prices for maize, millet, rice, and sorghum; their low/high values remain blank. Several other country/crop series have fewer than five years. The exact years are recorded in `prices.csv`, rather than presented as a complete five-year range. Costs outside the U.S., county yields, and custom rates outside Iowa are absent. These gaps prevent complete money results for many crops and locations.

`CN` uses FAOSTAT's **China, mainland** reporting area. Country references stay national; they are not province or field estimates. The FAOSTAT extracts retain U.S. observations, but output rows prefer NASS wherever an equivalent U.S. crop reference exists. This prevents unit choice from depending on CSV row order.

All NASS and FAOSTAT yields have `area_basis=harvested`; all ERS costs have `area_basis=planted`. Both use conventional `/acre` or `/ha` unit strings, so the optional `area_basis` column must be preserved by the loader. A harvested-acre yield cannot be combined with a planted-acre cost without an explicit compatible basis. The backend leaves such margins blank with a reason. No planted-to-harvested ratio is assumed. Source years and populations also differ; users should replace references with their own compatible figures.

Some source commodities are broader than the season-specific crop catalog. `wheat_all`, `barley_all`, `oats_all`, `rye_all`, `rapeseed_all`, and `canola_all` retain those distinctions. `cotton_all`, `cotton_upland`, `cotton_lint`, and `seed_cotton` are separate. In particular, the catalog alias `wheat` resolves to spring wheat, so aggregate wheat references deliberately use `wheat_all`. Aggregate references are not automatically substituted for winter or spring crop IDs. Dry pulse species likewise remain separate rather than being relabelled as generic `pulses`.

Bushels are kept as bushels. Sorghum's NASS price is per cwt while its NASS yield is per bushel, so the loader cannot calculate revenue from that pair without an unsupported test-weight conversion. Rice and sunflower use mass units, for which exact mass conversion is possible. Price and yield mismatches remain visible gaps.

FAO observations flagged `E` (estimated) or `I` (imputed) receive `origin=dataset_estimate`; other published source observations receive `verified_source`. Source flags remain in the extracts and locators. Calculated range statistics and unit conversions are documented in the locator. These labels describe the evidence, not certainty about a farm's outcome.

## Rebuild and verify

Run `python3 backend/data/economics/import_sources.py` from the repository root to rebuild the four CSVs, `coverage.json`, and `gaps.json` from the committed source extracts. This uses only the Python standard library and does not access the network.

To reproduce extraction from the original downloads, install `pdfplumber` and run `python3 backend/data/economics/import_sources.py --refresh /tmp/acre-economics-cache`. The command downloads absent files into that cache and checks their SHA-256 hashes against the pinned manifest before extracting data. If an upstream file changes, extraction stops for review. A cache containing the pinned original downloads also works offline. Source updates require reviewing the years, mappings, publication edition, and retrieval date before repinning; this command does not silently adopt a newer publication.

Verification against the backend implementation in the NSA26 checkout loaded all 617 rows with zero loader issues. Economics checks covered all ten countries, state selection in NC and Iowa, no Iowa-rate fallback to NC, U.S. NASS unit preference, missing Argentina/India prices, and blank margins for incompatible area bases. The economics test suite is run separately from the reproducible data build.
